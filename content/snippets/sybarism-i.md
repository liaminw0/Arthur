**Sybarism I (2026) by Arthur Hovenkamp**

As I am waiting at the front door of the apartment complex I am looking at every passer-by. I am about to have a viewing of the apartment I am planning to move into. Despite it being a viewing it is not going to be a viewing of a potential home, but a viewing of a definite home. Whether or not it is up to my standards does not matter, the availability is too scarce.

“35m² with two rooms.”

“Private bathroom with toilet and shower cabin.”

“Private kitchen with combination microwave.”

“An additional 5m² of storage space.”

“€950,- per month, three months paid in advance. Excluding €120,- service charges.”

It is going to have to do.

Every passer-by could be the agent of the rental agency. Uniforms are not to be expected, neither is the garment of a janitor. Although those blue of collar know the apartments and could tell me most about it, those white of collar tend to handle the viewings. They are representatives of the organisation, nothing more and nothing less.

I expect the rental agent to have an approachable yet corporate outfit. Heels cannot be expected, white sneakers most definitely. As the rental agency primarily targets young people like students and expats. Neither can dresses and skirts be expected, just as in the case with heels this would be too formal. I am sure she is going to wear jeans, most likely a loose fit. She will definitely be wearing a coloured blouse. Most likely blue, somewhere between navy and cobalt, as during her training as a rental agent she was told blue emits authority. She does want to emit authority, even more than approachability, as she is ambitious. Why else would she degrade herself to represent an agency if not to decide what the agency represents?

While all this is true there are always wild cards in play, but in this field of occupation such people are rare. Extremely rare. I see a woman walking towards the door. She has jeans on, but with a baggy fit. Under it she has black boots, above a purple tank top showing off a patchwork of tattoos on her left arm. She walks past me into the building, putting her  white key fob to the scanner next to the door. She quickly glances at me, most likely to be sure I don’t enter the building as an uninvited mystery guest. She must be a resident.

No, I am sure. The representative wears white sneakers, paired with jeans and a blue blouse.

A woman of this description is walking towards the entrance of the apartment complex. She is holding a Space Gray iPad.

It must be her.

She sees me and is coming towards me with a smile.

It is her.

“Hello! Are you here for the viewing?”, she says with an even brighter and whiter smile.

“Hello. Yes, I am.”

“Good! What is your name?”

“My first name is Cadmus, you can call me Cade. My surname is…”

“Great! Can you check and sign this?”

“Yes, I can.”

She hands me her iPad. I see a document with my information as well as legal information. I recognise it from an e-mail from the agency. Just to make sure I want to read it again.

“The document was included with the sign-up e-mail, you should have read it already!”

She cannot hide her passive-aggressiveness behind her smile.

“Have I? Are you sure it is the same document?”

“I am sure of it! You can check it later!”

“But you asked me to sign as well as check the document, right? Do you mean I can sign it later as well?”

“No! Otherwise I cannot show you the apartment!”

“Can you at least tell me what is in the document?”

“You have already read it!”

She exhausts me.

“Fine.”

I sign the document and move on. She smiles, but does not say the expected “Thank you!”. She puts the iPad back in her black leather dome bag and takes out an orange key fob. She opens the door and we enter.

The lobby has white popcorn walls and oak coloured laminate flooring. Apart from a cluster of cherry red mailboxes next to the entrance there is a stairwell, an elevator shaft and a single door most likely leading to the storage units. The building has the smell of recently unpackaged furniture combined with a slight sense of spoilage.

We take the stairs. This could be for two reasons. First of all, if an elevator would be late, expectedly dirty, or malfunctioning, it could leave a bad impression on me. Second of all, it could have to do with the agency’s willingness to make a healthy impression. There is hardly any chance taking the stairs is her choice.

We traversed twelve flights of stairs to reach the sixth floor.

The hallway is dark. Apart from the windows through the stairwell there are no windows. To the left there is a short corridor leading to the elevator shaft, with an imitation palm tree in the corner and an information poster.  Information meaning the agency’s rules and contact information, with a QR-code at the bottom. To the right there is another short corridor leading to an emergency exit door, blocked off with another imitation palm tree.

As she opens the third apartment to the left a bright, white light enters the hallway. Light cascades of the popcorn walls and polished laminate flooring.

“The apartment is 35 square meter in total. On the left we have the bathroom, with a toilet and a shower cabin. On the right we have the kitchen, with a combination microwave built in. Through the kitchen we have the living room and to the left the door to the bedroom. Do you have any questions?”

I decide to walk back to the kitchen. Its width is that of three cupboards above and below, with a sink in the middle. The combination microwave is in the above right cupboard, with directly under it an induction cooktop. It is white and chrome. The counter is made of embossed metal. The bathroom is not interesting either. Nor are the living room and the bedroom. The windows can barely be opened.

“Yes, I do have some questions.”

“Go ahead!”

“Am I allowed to modify the apartment?”

“You should have read that already, but yes! You can personalize it! As long as you return the apartment back to us in its original state!”

“Excluding modifications the kitchen, any doors and windows, right?”

“Yes, that’s right! As you should have read already!”

“Good, then I would like to accept the apartment.”

“Great!”

She grabs her iPad again and hands it to me. It shows a rental contract, but already scrolled to the end where I have to put my signature.

“You will have to sign this document then!”

“Then I will read it, as I have not read it before.”

“Go ahead!”, she says with an annoyed voice. Rolling her eyes back and walking towards the window.

I read the document not because I want to read it, but because I want to put her in her place. Not because I want to strike her down, but to show here how unbecoming and dehumanizing her career is. I can only usurp her cobalt authority with reading what there is to read, by doing what I can do, while there is little to be done.

There is not that much interesting in the contract, except for the ban of overnight guests and musical instruments. It also says that violations will be punished with a fine of an additional €150,- per violation added to the rent of next month. Quite interesting, as enforcing such bans is illegal according to the law. However, pressing charges against the agency is not an option for the residents. The agency is very much allowed to evict anyone they want in three months due. Despite charges being pressed would succeed almost certainly.

This is not a viewing of a potential home, but a viewing of a definite home. It has to do.

I sign the contract.

\

A few days later I am on my way to the apartment again. This time I am going there with a rented Iveco Daily packed with my possessions. I moved out some years ago, at the exact same time the COVID-19 pandemic started. I moved into a closed down city, all alone, without chances to meet anyone. Two years later I moved back to my parents, at the exact same time the pandemic ended. I lingered for four years, to move out again now. Having moved out before has its advantages, possessing basic furniture already.

I am not alone, two of my friends are with me. We occupy the three seats of the van.

“Zach, are you okay with the music?”, says Elissa to her boyfriend.

“What do you think?”, responds Zach, as the three of us break out into laughing.

The driver’s seat is occupied by Zach, short for Zachary. He is one of my best friends. I met him in kindergarten and we have been friends on and off ever since. We grew up in the same neighbourhood, where he still lives, with Elissa. She is sitting to the right of him, between the two of us. She also grew up in the same neighbourhood, but Zach got to know her in high school. During our middle to late teens vwe grew apart, although we kept playing Minecraft together. Elissa would eventually join our server and slowly but steadily take his attention away from me. I despised them for quite a while. But when I returned to my parents’ after COVID, seeing the two together, helping them move in together, I forgave them.

“Cade, do you already know what you are going to do with your bed?”

There is a huge dark grey boxspring in the back of the van. My parents did not want the colossus to remain in their house. I don’t like it either. My grandmother gifted it to me when she heard I fell in love with a girl. Nothing ever came of it, but years of mutual longing and unrequited love. It is too large, too grey and too much of a burden to keep.

“We could go to a second hand shop and drop it off?”

“So you want to sleep on the couch?”

“Why not? It is a sofa bed.”

“I know there is a antique on the outskirts. Let me look it up!”

“I see, the two of you want to go shopping again.”, says Zach while defeatedly leaning on the steering wheel.

Admittedly I am hoping to find a bed up to my standards at the antique shop. My sofa bed is quite nice. Made from a wooden frame, polished at the arm rests and legs, with turquoise corduroy cushioning. There is a handle at the bottom you can pull, making the couch into a king sized bed.

The antique shop occupies a warehouse in the city’s industrial area. We park near the drop-off area. The staff wholeheartedly accept the colossus. Despite it being barely a decade old and far from antique

“Elissa, I have a funny thought. You know how most antique shops sell, to say it rudely, absolute garbage, right?”

“Yes, of course! Fast fashion, polyester carpets, and such. You really have to look for something nice.”

“Indeed, but the nicest stuff must go somewhere, right? I think this antique shop sells the nicest stuff, not just the antique, but of course for a price.”

We open the door and immediately I am struck with the fact that my funny thought was a correct thought. In the middle of the shop is a Volvo P210 DUETT, with decorative lanterns strung to the roof rack going to the ceiling of the warehouse. Around it a summer-oriented thematic scene with vintage surfboards and skateboards.

I am specifically drawn to a pile of boxes at the shoes section. I open one of them and find a pair of beige Onitsuka Tiger Mexico 66’s, with a red crossed side-stripes logo at the sides. They are in a pristine condition while being made in the late 70’s. Before Onitsuka’s revival by Asics in 2002. They were of a better quality back then, but they are also thrice the price compared to today. Looking at the pile of boxes it must be worth about three years worth of rent. Give or take a few guests, instruments and forthcoming violations.

I have lost Elissa, while we left Zach at the car. He will most likely stay there until we are done shopping. There is a lot to see in the shop, but we have quite some moving to do. I decide to take a quick look at the beds and there is one that peaks my interest. It is a rough grey metal frame. Quite barebones, simple and as of yet without a mattress. Different from my colossal boxspring it does in has room for storage under it. I decide to buy it, but first I will need help getting it to the van.

Close to the checkout I get uncomfortable. The girl with the purple tank top, now wearing a black tank top and a purple skirt, is behind the counter. She sees me walking towards her, she recognizes me and becomes visibly uncomfortable.

“Hello, I am interested in buying one of the bedframes. The grey one, although I will paint it most likely. I am not sure what colour yet though. I thought it said €40,55?”

“No, that one is €40,95. We don’t label our price like that. Pay by card?”

“Sure, my friends will help me carry it to our car.”

“Alright.”, she says before abruptly reorganizing the cash register.

I cannot find Elissa in the shop. At the car I find Zach, as well as Elissa.

“There you are!”

“Zach, can you help me with carrying a new bedframe to the van?”

“Sure, but why do you seem so distressed?”

“Oh, it is nothing. Shall we go now?”

“Alright. Elissa, will you join us in the shop?”

“No, I know myself, when I go back there hours will pass!”

We carry the bed with ease. It is quite light, it is just awkward to carry alone. The awkwardly social Zach manages to arrange that we can pass through the workshop instead of going all around the warehouse to get to the van.

“Hey, aren’t you the guy that just dropped off the grey boxspring bed?”

“Yeah?”, I answer while I put the bedframe down.

“That does seem like quite the downgrade!”

“The bed was just too colossal for my liking.”

“I see. Well, you can have something on the house!”

“Really, anything?”

“As long as it is not too expensive!”

“Alright. Well, I did see Onitsuka’s of my size, but I suppose those are…”

“Take them! We cannot really sell them anyway!”

“I cannot accept that…”

“Just take them!”, says Zach.

“Yes, otherwise I will take offense!”

“Sure, thank you!”

“You are welcome! We had a sealed top mattress of exactly that size for ages now. Meaning we can sell your bed basically as a brand new king-sized boxspring!”

“Ah, that explains it. Again, thank you very much!”

“You are welcome! See you again soon I hope!”

I see the girl typing away on her phone behind the counter. She sees me and quickly looks away again. She is certainly messaging her friends about her stalker. I take the box I held in my hands earlier and walk out of the door. The girl does not take her eyes away from the screen as we do so. I remain a stalker and not a stalking shoplifter. Zach does not notice anything.

We arrive at the apartment, we park the Iveco in front of the entrance. Before anything we put a plastic cover over the laminate flooring. I had to move at once, my furniture needed a place to stay, but paintwork still needs to be done, albeit with furniture put in one room and then the other. Other than that it is perfect. Elissa started painting the living room, Zach and I can carry the furniture about ten meter from the van towards the elevator, go up, walk another ten meter, and put it down all atop of each other in the bedroom The same for the in total nine boxes of kitchen appliances and cutlery, clothing and sheets, as well as miscellaneous items.

At the end of the day, eating pizza while sitting on table seats in the middle of the living room, about half of the living room has been painted and every piece of furniture and box cramped into the bedroom.

“Will your parents be helping you tomorrow?”

“Zach, you know this is not the time for this question!”, answers Elissa.

“Elissa, Zach is right to ask. And no, they won’t be helping me tomorrow or some other day. Mom is busy with my absent sister, while dad is working on the damaged garage door. But Elissa is right, I don’t really want to talk about it.”

My younger sister ran away after an argument with my parents over someone she was dating. The two of us were not on bad terms, but my father obliged me and our brother to look for her. We saw her, she saw us, and understood what was going on. She got away and blocked us. This happened three years ago. Ever since my mother has been grieving, always being on the lookout for her. My father becoming more and more paranoid, attributing a car accidentally hitting our garage door to an attack by her daughter’s boyfriend. Although it is not clear if it was a man she was dating to begin with.

“Damn, sorry Cade. I would like to help you, but we are just too busy.”

“I could report sick in a few days.”

“Ellie, but your new employer…”

“Zach, I can handle it.”

Zach and Elissa have gone home. I am alone in my new home. The walls in the living room have been painted with a red terracotta paint, preparing for large walnut coloured plinths to be added to the ceiling, corners and flooring. To the floor there will be added tapestries to unify the warmness of colour against the coldness of the sterile laminate. Moreover, the base tones of earthly red and brown combined with the beige of the oaken flooring bring forth an interesting complementary set of colours. More is remained to be mused upon, but before such matters I need to take the pizza boxes out to the trash and go to sleep in the cramped bedroom. On my coach nevertheless, as I am only in the possession of a bedframe. To break in my new shoes I decide to put them on.

The nightly halls of the apartment complex are lighted by motion detection lights. Going through the hallway, passing the dual palm trees and waiting for the elevator I can hear all kinds of sounds from the sixth floor. Exposed piping from the seventh floor can be seen traversing their way unto ours, going down through the stairwell.

The lift does work.

We cannot depose paper in the underground containers in front of the building, but next to the small parking garage next to the storage units on the ground floor behind the sole lobby door. There is no light, I have to use the flashlight of my phone to traverse the depths of these vaults. I hear an animal scurrying through the tight corridors. It is a stone marten. Its eyes flicker dimly, when it is standing in front of the blue paper recycling container. It is bulging with all kinds of waste, not just boxes. I put the pizza boxes on top and return to my room.

At the end of the hallway of the sixth floor I hear a door being opened. Momentarily I halt to open my own, curiously waiting to meet a neighbour of mine. As she exists I realize who she is, the tattooed girl with the purple skirt. We see each other, startled she return to her room and I return to mine.

I run toward my couch and for hours struggle to fall asleep.
