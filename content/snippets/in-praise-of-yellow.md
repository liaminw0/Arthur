**In
Praise of Yellow (2026) Arthur Hovenkamp\
**Last week I
found myself yearning for a change of colours in the trees of the woods next to
my home. For the moment when the monotonous greens start to shift, but before
they turn red and brown and brittle. I was yearning for them to turn yellow,
the brightest of the primary colours, the most grand of them furthermore. Especially
in its duality. For what other colour symbolizes cowardice in the face of
change, while also symbolizing hope for change to come? For what other colour
symbolizes sickness and decay, as well as happiness? For what other colour was
used to marginalise Jews in Europe, while at the same time in dynastic China
for the emperor alone? Perhaps this duality is best expressed by the decadent
movement in France and Brittain, by writers such as Oscar Wilde and Paul Verlaine
and painters such as Aubrey Beardsley and Gustave Moreau, who took the colour
yellow, and the wording *decadence*, meaning ‘the act of decay’, to show
off their movement as a sickness against the bourgeois art world of their time.
Perhaps this is not even the greatest parallel, as what creature is more ridden
with impossible dualities than humanity itself? Us as creatures claiming to be
knowing whether or not true knowledge is possible to be had. Us as creatures
being brave enough to claim we know anything, while being too craven to admit
otherwise. Us as creatures hoping for true knowledge to be had at some point,
and rejoicing in joy when we claim we have come closer towards it. And perhaps
especially us as a sickness, as decay, being creatures of nature but unable to
do anything but to make it fester. Oh, how great, how sublime, the colour
yellow is!
